## Overview

**DataEcho** is an end-to-end framework for recording, storing and replaying immersive XR sessions. A Unity package captures what a person did in a headset (where they moved, where they looked, what they touched), streams it to Elasticsearch, and a web app replays the session in 3D in the browser, from the person's own point of view or from outside, with no headset needed. An MCP gateway lets an LLM query the same data in plain language.

It was built at the Emerging Analytics Center (EAC Lab, UA Little Rock) under Dr. Aryabrata Basu, and it is the data backbone for my research on reflective telemetry replay.

## Why

Watching an XR study live, or reviewing a 2D screen recording, shows very little of what actually happened in 3D: where someone hesitated, where they looked just before choosing, how their path folded back on itself. Recordings also scale badly once there are dozens of sessions, and the participants themselves rarely get to revisit their own performance. DataEcho keeps the *data* instead of the video, so a session can be replayed, measured and compared long after it happened.

## How It Works

```
 Unity app (Quest)        Backend            Storage            Replay + analysis
 -----------------        -------            -------            -----------------
 DataEcho Logger  ──▶  FastAPI (auth,  ──▶  Elasticsearch  ──▶  React + Babylon.js replay
 pose, gaze,           app keys,                                MCP gateway for LLMs
 controllers,          analytics, ML)
 object states,
 events
```

- **Logger:** a Unity package. Drop in a prefab and it records head, hand and eye poses, controller input, object states and custom events at 10 Hz. A batch that can't be uploaded is retried and then parked on disk and re-sent on the next launch, so a Wi-Fi drop or taking the headset off doesn't cost the end of a session.
- **Backend:** a FastAPI service with login and refresh tokens, per-app keys for Unity apps, session analytics and prediction models.
- **Storage:** Elasticsearch, so a session is searchable and aggregable, not just playable.
- **Web replay:** React and Babylon.js. Scrub a session on a timeline, switch between first-person and third-person cameras, show the eye-gaze ray and gaze points, see the controllers' buttons, triggers and grips as they were pressed, and draw the path of any tracked object.
- **MCP gateway:** a Model Context Protocol server exposing session data to an LLM: list apps and sessions, search, count and aggregate in Elasticsearch, summarise a session, measure how far an object moved, and analyse backtracking and decision confidence.

## Research

DataEcho started as a way to study XR usability and grew into the subject of its own paper.

- **IEEE VR 2025 Abstracts and Workshops.** [*Gaze Insights in XR: Real-Time Eye-Tracking Analytics with Elasticsearch*](https://api.atitkharel.com.np/portfolio/atit/pdf?id=10.1109/vrw66409.2025.00028) showed the pipeline: recording any XR interaction, storing it in an ELK stack, and replaying it through DataEcho. It was presented as a [poster](https://api.atitkharel.com.np/portfolio/atit/pdf?id=2025_expo_poster) at the 2025 Student Research and Creative Works Expo at UA Little Rock, where it won Best Poster. ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10972667/))
- **IEEE VR 2026 Abstracts and Workshops.** [*DataEcho: Reflective Telemetry Replay for Studying Navigation Strategy Refinement in XR*](https://api.atitkharel.com.np/portfolio/atit/pdf?id=10.1109/vrw70859.2026.00057) (first author) adds a controlled study: 25 participants did two VR navigation sessions a day apart, with or without reviewing a replay of their first run. Replay-review led to a significant increase in continuous movement, and directional improvements in backtracking and head-orientation stability. Path optimality did not reliably improve in the low-redundancy layout used. ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11489749/))
